Where a $14.9M retailer makes and loses money

Four years of order data from an office supplies retailer (Superstore sample, Canada, 2009 to 2012), cleaned in Python, modelled as a star schema and analysed in Power BI. This page summarises what the data shows.

At a glance

Key findings

The four findings a manager would act on first.

  1. Furniture: 35% of sales, 2.3% marginTechnology earns 14.8% and Office Supplies 13.8%. A third of revenue brings in under 8% of profit.
  2. Tables and Bookcases sell at a lossTables lose $99K and Bookcases $34K, despite being the 2nd and 8th largest sub-categories by sales.
  3. Margin falls as discounts rise13.4% margin at 0 to 3% discount, 6.4% at 10% or more.
  4. Express shipping isn't fasterExpress Air ships in 2.0 days on average, the same as Regular Air.

Recommendations

  1. Reprice or limit discounts on Tables and Bookcases. They turn the most revenue into losses.
  2. Require approval for discounts of 10% or more, where margin is less than half the low-discount level.
  3. Review the Express Air premium. It delivers no faster than Regular Air.
  4. Investigate falling order values. Order counts are flat, so the sales decline comes from smaller orders.
